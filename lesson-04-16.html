Урок 16 (Модуль 4) — ⚕️ Morning report: разбор осложнения (M&M, 20.08)

Сцена: утренняя конференция, 20 врачей. Ты докладываешь свой случай: мужчина 58 лет, острый панкреатит, на третий день — септический шок, ИВЛ. Ретроспективно: антибиотики начали на 18 часов позже, чем следовало.

Задача урока: признать ошибку профессионально — не оправдываясь и не занимаясь самобичеванием. Грамматика в бою: should have + V3, третий тип условных.

Оппоненты: Dr. Weiss (заведующий, ведёт конференцию), Dr. Patel (из зала, резко).

🎬 Полный диалог — как он прошёл

Dr. Weiss: Okay, next case. Eduard, this is yours — the pancreatitis from Tuesday. Go ahead.

Ты: Thank you Dr. Weiss. As you mentioned earlier, with the case of a 58-year-old male who was admitted with a clinical picture of acute pancreatitis. On admission, he was stable. I mean: Blood pressure, Kidney function and Respiration. At that stage, it seemed like simple acute pancreatitis. Anything was different. So he was treated by our protocol in this kind of disease. He was under supportive care. CT scan didn't reveal any obvious cause of pancreatitis, gallbladder was normal, with no signs of cholangitis, or malignancies. We didn't start him on antibiotics. Later, on day three, he suddenly deteriorated to septic shock, and we had to put him on a ventilator. The question is: if we had started antibiotics earlier, would we have prevented his deterioration? On the one hand, the protocol said for us not to give him antibiotics. On the other hand, he deteriorated quickly, and on antibiotics, it wouldn't happen.

Dr. Weiss: Alright. So let me push on that a bit. You said the protocol didn't call for antibiotics — fair. But what was his lactate on day two? And did anything change overnight that should have made you reconsider?
Dr. Patel: (резко) And honestly — eighteen hours is a long time. Was anyone actually watching the trend, or did it only get picked up on the morning round?

Ты: Good question, Dr. Weiss. To be honest, lactate was overlooked, its importance cannot be minimized. We had to pay more attention to lactate. We should have checked it often. Dr. Patel, I can't disagree with you. Eighteen hours is a long time.

Dr. Weiss: Good. That's the honest answer, and it's the useful one. So — what changes? Give me something concrete the unit does differently from Monday.
Dr. Patel: (мягче) And for what it's worth — I've seen this exact scenario twice. The protocol has a blind spot.

Ты: First of all, we have to change our protocol. We need to fit it to the patients. Dr. Patel is right. The protocol has a blind spot. I'll check the literature and try to find something new about it. And the second thing is our routine. We should to change it. I mean abnormal blood tests. We should build a mechanism on how we notify the senior doctor about flagged tests.

✍️ Тот же доклад — как сказал бы носитель

Доклад:
This is a 58-year-old male admitted Tuesday with acute pancreatitis. On admission he was stable — pressure, renal function, respiratory status all holding. At that stage it looked like straightforward pancreatitis. Nothing was out of the ordinary.
He was managed per our protocol — supportive care, no antibiotics. CT showed no obvious cause, gallbladder normal, no cholangitis, no malignancy.
On day three he went into septic shock and we had to put him on a ventilator.
Looking back, the gap was 18 hours. His lactate started climbing at midnight on day two, and we didn't act on it until the following afternoon.
The protocol supported our decision — but the protocol isn't the whole story. We should have escalated when the lactate turned.

На критику:
Fair question. His lactate was 2.1 on admission and 4.6 by midnight on day two. Nobody flagged it overnight — it was picked up on the morning round, and by then he was already tipping.
Dr. Patel — I can't disagree. Eighteen hours is a long time. We should have had a trigger on that trend, and we didn't. That's on us.
If we'd escalated when the lactate turned, this might have gone differently.

Что меняется:
Two things.
First — from Monday, any lactate above 4 gets called to the senior on call, day or night. That we can do immediately, no protocol change needed.
Second, the protocol itself. Dr. Patel is right — there's a blind spot for patients who are trending badly but don't meet criteria. I'll go through the literature and bring a proposal to next week's meeting.
And Dr. Patel — if you've seen this twice, I'd like to look at those cases with you. Three is a pattern.

💡 Последняя фраза превращает критика в соавтора. Самый сильный ход на M&M.

Цифры и моменты не названы ни разу. В M&M это читается хуже самой ошибки — коллеги видят не ошибку, а нежелание её конкретизировать.

📌 Правило: называй цифру и называй момент. «Мы недосмотрели» = размывание. «2.1 на поступлении, 4.6 к полуночи, заметили утром» = профессионал.

⚠️ Это та же линия, что и в small talk (уроки 13–15): там пропускались «лишние» вопросы, здесь — неудобные. Механизм один: отвечать только на то, что сам считаешь главным.

🎯 Вторая находка: риторический вопрос вместо признания

В докладе ни разу не прозвучало «we should have» — хотя это была прямая заготовка. Вместо этого — риторический вопрос: «if we had started antibiotics earlier, would we have prevented...?»

Вопрос безопаснее — он не требует признания.

📌 Формула для M&M:

«The protocol supported X. In hindsight, X was the wrong call for this patient.»

Не «мы следовали правилам», а «правила не спасли конкретно его».

⚠️ И might, не would: this might have gone differently. В M&M категоричность = слабость позиции.
